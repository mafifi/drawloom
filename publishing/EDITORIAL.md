# Editorial voice

Write so a curious business reader can follow without knowing the technology.
Simple language and honest claims build trust. Technical readers should find
precision, not a separate layer of jargon.

This guide applies to articles, video scripts, captions and presentations.
The visual system belongs in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

## Voice

- Use everyday words, short sentences and short paragraphs. Vary the rhythm so
  the prose still sounds like a person, not a sequence of slogans.
- Write firsthand accounts in the author's voice. Do not invent experiences,
  dialogue, motives or results to make a story more compelling.
- Start with the work, the problem and why it matters. Introduce the technology
  when it helps explain the story.
- Explain an idea before naming its technical term. Use the term only when it
  earns its place. Do not make readers learn our internal vocabulary.
- Prefer concrete examples and active verbs to abstract claims. Say “see what
  the agent did” before reaching for “observability”.
- Be direct and warm. Avoid hype, corporate language, sweeping claims about
  what everyone does, and repeated “not X, but Y” constructions.
- Keep necessary nuance. Short writing is not permission to omit a limitation
  or turn an estimate into a fact.

## Consistent terms

- A **workbench** is the person's working surface: the developing result,
  relevant material and controls for directing the work.
- A **harness** supplies the agent's instructions, context, tools and controls.
- **Drawloom** provides reusable capabilities for building those systems.
  It is not synonymous with a particular workbench or model provider.
- Keep provider conversation history distinct from Drawloom memory, and model
  inference distinct from an interactive agent session. Use the exact capability
  names from ADR 0005 in technical figures, with a short plain-language explanation.

## Evidence and visuals


- Separate what happened, what the author thinks it means, and what remains
  untested. Label estimates and imagined alternatives in the prose itself.
- Link primary sources near factual product claims. Product similarities do
  not establish shared internal architecture.
- Use screenshots to show real work and diagrams to explain relationships.
  Label proposed screens and illustrative comparisons. Never pass a mockup off
  as evidence of a working product.
- Keep source checks, unresolved questions and visual plans beside each piece,
  outside the reader-facing article. Do not add empty files for unused formats.
- Drafting, previewing and committing are not approval to publish.

## Before review

Read it aloud. Replace words you would not use in conversation. Remove repeated
points. Check that every paragraph helps the reader understand the work, the
lesson or the next question. Keep useful detail; cut the effort needed to read it.
